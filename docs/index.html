What this lab is about

Pretrained audio models such as CLAP already know what a microwave or typing sounds like. What they cannot do is tell which microwave beeped, name sounds that are specific to one room or missing from their training ontology, or stay accurate on far-field audio from a microphone across the room. Teaching them those sounds normally means a person labelling recordings, or answering "what was that sound?" for every new class.

Our project replaces that person with a camera. A fixed camera with an open-vocabulary object detector names what is happening, and a spatial gate checks each name before it is trusted: a microphone array measures the direction the sound came from, and a 3-D map of the room (from a depth camera) says whether that direction leads to the named object, to an echo off a wall, or to something the camera cannot see. Only verified labels train the audio model, and it is compared directly against CLAP on the sounds CLAP misses.

How long will this take?

Setup takes about 30 minutes. Each concept page takes 30 to 45 minutes to read properly with the demos and questions. Budget a week for the six core pages alongside your other commitments, then a few days for the five project pages.

What you need to know already

  • Python and NumPy: functions, arrays, slicing, and reading other people's code.
  • Basic linear algebra: vectors, dot products, matrix multiplication.
  • A little calculus and trigonometry: sines and cosines, logarithms. The pages explain everything else, including complex numbers where they appear.
  • Some PyTorch helps but is not required.
