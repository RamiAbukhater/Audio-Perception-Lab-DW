How to reach out

  1. Read at least one of the listed papers properly, and skim the lab's recent publications page.
  2. Keep the email short: four to six sentences. Who you are (UCSD student, DataWorks Audio Perception Lab, your module); one specific connection between their work and your project ("we are using GCC-PHAT and seeing reflection-locked errors similar to those in your 2017 paper"); one concrete ask.
  3. Make the ask small and specific: a 20-minute conversation, feedback on one design choice, pointers to a dataset, or whether a student in their group would be open to chatting. Do not open by asking to join the lab.
  4. Attach or link something real: a one-page project summary or a figure from your results, not a CV.
  5. Use your UCSD email, a clear subject line ("Question about sparse DOA from a DataWorks student project"), and follow up once after a week if you hear nothing.
  6. Graduate students and postdocs in these labs are often easier to reach and just as helpful. Lab pages list them.
